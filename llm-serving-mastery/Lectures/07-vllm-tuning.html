--max-num-seqs: the batch ceiling

Topic 07 · draft

Smaller (e.g. 32)

Shorter TPOT ticks (fewer requests competing per step). But earlier admission blocking — the queue forms sooner.

Larger (e.g. 256)

Better weight amortization (more requests share each pass). But the tail grows: more concurrent decode work per step.

This is the Round 03 knob. The course precedent: max-num-seqs=64 with FLASH_ATTN raised the bar by 2.3 percent. Connect to the batching economy from Topic 06.

What the mock said about the batch cap

Topic 07 · draft

the mock sweep

five declared configs, one run — read the record for numbers: cutting the cap beat default on both axes (default: 2377.1 tok/s, 860 ms). Cutting the cap shortened each request's queue wait so much that completed throughput rose.

Why the free lunch is suspicious

The mock charges a quadratic queue penalty per effective batch; a real engine amortizes compute differently. Treat the direction as the lesson, not the magnitude.

Mock-calibrated search, single run (seminars/runs/2026-10-26-topic07-search-mock-v2/README.md). The teaching point: sometimes one knob moves both axes the right way — and the honest response is to ask WHICH mechanism paid, not to celebrate.

--gpu-memory-utilization: the KV pool share

Topic 07 · draft

More (e.g. 0.9)

Larger KV pool — more resident requests before preemption kicks in. The memory knee moves right.

Less (e.g. 0.85)

More VRAM for activations and workspace. But the KV pool shrinks — preemption arrives earlier under load.

Connect to the KV budget arithmetic from Topic 06. The 125-request ceiling was computed at a specific utilization. Change the share, change the ceiling.

--max-model-len: the context declaration

Topic 07 · draft

Generous (e.g. 4096)

Any client may arrive with a long prompt. The scheduler plans for the worst case — reserved but possibly wasted.

Tight (e.g. 1024)

Plan for what actually arrives. But a legitimate long prompt gets rejected — a product decision, not a tuning one.

This knob teaches the difference between planning for the maximum and planning for the observed distribution. The frozen corpus bins (A-short, B-long) make this concrete.

Chunked prefill: slicing the enemy

Topic 07 · draft

Without chunking

A new request's prefill takes the whole step. Every resident request waits. TPOT jumps — the Topic 06 waterfall showed this.

With chunking

The prefill is sliced across multiple steps. Decode keeps earning tokens. The cost: slightly longer total prefill latency.

This is the scheduler interference fix from Topic 06 now as a knob. The waterfall's grey bands narrow for residents when chunking is on.

Why you cannot try everything

Topic 07 · draft

Combinatorics

4 knobs × several values each × 4 waves × 3 repeats = hundreds of GPU-hours. The budget is finite.

Predeclared search

Enumerate a small space. One-factor-at-a-time first, then a confirmatory pair. Write the plan BEFORE running.

This is the methodological core. A search that keeps rerunning until it likes the result is not a search. Round 03 precedent: the rejected Triton candidate stays rejected.

Why wave 64 is the comparison point

Topic 07 · draft

The judge scores 32 and 64

The leaderboard takes the geometric mean of throughput at waves 32 and 64 — tuning for wave 128 alone optimizes a number nobody scores.

Near the knee, not past it

Wave 64 sits where the interesting behavior lives for this workload. Wave 128 is retained: does the config move where collapse begins?

The decision uses 64-client evidence; the collapse question uses 128. Two different questions, two different waves — say this out loud or students tune for the wrong target.

Reading the table: the Pareto front

Topic 07 · draft

No best on everything

One config is fastest but has the longest tail. Another is gentle but slow. The front is the set where nothing dominates.

The SLO picks the point

Not "highest throughput" but "highest throughput that still clears the SLO." The front shows the trade; the SLO is your value judgement.

Pareto dominance: config A dominates B iff A is at least as fast AND at least as gentle. The front is what survives. Students implement pareto_front() to find it.

Pareto dominance, formally

Topic 07 · draft
$$A \succ B \iff u(A) \geq u(B) \;\wedge\; u(A) \neq u(B)$$
\(u(k)\)pairthr(k) and minus p95(k), wave 64
\(\geq\)componentwiseAt least as fast AND at least as gentle
\(\neq\)strictnessAt least one strictly better - no exact ties
In the recorded mock sweep pair-seqs32-chunked came out on top — read the record, not the slide. The front may hold one or two points depending on the run — read it, do not memorize it.
Dominance with an error-rate column needs the gate form: configs failing the error SLO are excluded before dominance is even assessed.
Students implement pareto_front() as a filter over undominated rows. Short function, long concept: this is the reading skill for every vendor benchmark they will ever see.

Held-out: the profile meets new texts

Topic 07 · draft

Same protocol

Same waves, same SLO, same journal fields. New texts from the V-split. One run. No policy change.

Three verdicts

Accept: clears SLO on new data. Reject: fails. Inconclusive: cannot distinguish healthy from lucky.

The freeze-to-held-out discipline is Topic 03's calibration-to-test split returning at the systems level. The experimental method scales from tensors to services.

Stretch: probe the edge on purpose

Topic 07 · draft

The edge test

Longest prompt in the corpus (817 tokens) against the smallest KV pool (mem 0.70). Served or rejected? The answer maps the failure boundary BEFORE production finds it.

The rollback plan

Trigger (metric + threshold + duration), action (revert / shed / page), evidence (what the on-call checks at 3 a.m.). Three TODOs, three sentences, zero dashboards invented.

The edge test and rollback plan are operational artifacts, not formalities. A plan without a trigger is hope; without action it is observation; without evidence it is unfollowable.

The knobs exist under other names

Topic 07 · draft

Same mechanisms

SGLang calls it max-running-requests; TensorRT-LLM folds it into max_batch_size; TGI speaks of max_concurrent_requests. The batching economy is the same equation.

What actually differs

Defaults, kernel efficiency, and which trade-offs are exposed. Porting a profile means re-deriving the knee on the new engine — never copying numbers.

The transferable asset is the METHOD: freeze a plan, run the same waves, read the Pareto front, freeze with a forecast. Numbers are engine-specific; discipline is portable.

The mock model: honest about what it is

Topic 07 · draft

Declared synthetic

Queue penalty, prefill rate and per-token cost are written in the notebook and calibrated once. It exists so the METHOD runs in minutes on CPU.

What it cannot tell you

Kernel realities, preemption storms, allocator fragmentation. That is what the GPU profile is for — same notebook, same protocol, measured numbers.

The mock/MOCK banner travels with every number it produces. A student who quotes mock throughput as engine throughput has failed the reading, not the running.

Speed knobs meet quality gates

Topic 07 · draft

The Round 03 precedent

A Triton candidate raised speed but failed the quality gate. It stays rejected — the search table keeps the row with its numbers and the one-sentence why.

Tuning is multi-objective

Speed, tail, errors, quality, cost. A knob that wins on one axis and loses on another is not a win; it is a trade the product owner must sign.

This is why the judge has non-speed gates from Round 02 frozen: tuning that improves throughput while degrading quality re-opens a settled round, and the burden of proof is on the change.
