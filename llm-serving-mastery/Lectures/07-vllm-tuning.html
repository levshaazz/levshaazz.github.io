<code>--max-num-seqs</code>: the batch ceiling

Topic 07 · draft

Smaller (e.g. 32)

Shorter TPOT ticks (fewer requests competing per step). But earlier admission blocking — the queue forms sooner.

Larger (e.g. 256)

Better weight amortization (more requests share each pass). But the tail grows: more concurrent decode work per step.

This is the Round 03 knob. The course precedent: max-num-seqs=64 with FLASH_ATTN raised the bar by 2.3 percent. Connect to the batching economy from Topic 06.

<code>--gpu-memory-utilization</code>: the KV pool share

Topic 07 · draft

More (e.g. 0.9)

Larger KV pool — more resident requests before preemption kicks in. The memory knee moves right.

Less (e.g. 0.85)

More VRAM for activations and workspace. But the KV pool shrinks — preemption arrives earlier under load.

Connect to the KV budget arithmetic from Topic 06. The 125-request ceiling was computed at a specific utilization. Change the share, change the ceiling.

<code>--max-model-len</code>: the context declaration

Topic 07 · draft

Generous (e.g. 4096)

Any client may arrive with a long prompt. The scheduler plans for the worst case — reserved but possibly wasted.

Tight (e.g. 1024)

Plan for what actually arrives. But a legitimate long prompt gets rejected — a product decision, not a tuning one.

This knob teaches the difference between planning for the maximum and planning for the observed distribution. The frozen corpus bins (A-short, B-long) make this concrete.

Chunked prefill: slicing the enemy

Topic 07 · draft

Without chunking

A new request's prefill takes the whole step. Every resident request waits. TPOT jumps — the Topic 06 waterfall showed this.

With chunking

The prefill is sliced across multiple steps. Decode keeps earning tokens. The cost: slightly longer total prefill latency.

This is the scheduler interference fix from Topic 06 now as a knob. The waterfall's grey bands narrow for residents when chunking is on.

Why you cannot try everything

Topic 07 · draft

Combinatorics

4 knobs × several values each × 4 waves × 3 repeats = hundreds of GPU-hours. The budget is finite.

Predeclared search

Enumerate a small space. One-factor-at-a-time first, then a confirmatory pair. Write the plan BEFORE running.

This is the methodological core. A search that keeps rerunning until it likes the result is not a search. Round 03 precedent: the rejected Triton candidate stays rejected.

Reading the table: the Pareto front

Topic 07 · draft

No best on everything

One config is fastest but has the longest tail. Another is gentle but slow. The front is the set where nothing dominates.

The SLO picks the point

Not "highest throughput" but "highest throughput that still clears the SLO." The front shows the trade; the SLO is your value judgement.

Pareto dominance: config A dominates B iff A is at least as fast AND at least as gentle. The front is what survives. Students implement pareto_front() to find it.

Among SLO-eligible front members, pick the fastest. Write the forecast. Freeze.
The freeze is the deliverable. A falsifiable choice with a written forecast is full marks even when held-out disappoints.
This is where the Round 03 precedent lands: the chosen profile is an experiment, not a guarantee. Held-out load arbitrates whether it generalizes.

Held-out: the profile meets new texts

Topic 07 · draft

Same protocol

Same waves, same SLO, same journal fields. New texts from the V-split. One run. No policy change.

Three verdicts

Accept: clears SLO on new data. Reject: fails. Inconclusive: cannot distinguish healthy from lucky. Evidence: seminars/runs/2026-10-20-topic07-search-mock/results.json.

The freeze-to-held-out discipline is Topic 03's calibration-to-test split returning at the systems level. The experimental method scales from tensors to services.
