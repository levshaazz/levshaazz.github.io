The Thermostat

Advanced Kubernetes: single-pod concurrency & HPA

One pod already serves a crowd — continuous batching holds the GPU at 90 % with an empty queue, so utilization is the anti-signal, and queue depth, KV pressure and TTFT are the honest signals. The HPA is one formula — desired = ceil(replicas × metric / target), clamped — guarded by an inclusive ±10 % tolerance band and asymmetric windows: scale-up now, scale-down after 300 s of maximum recommendations. A new pod pays the 165 s boot, so scale-down is cautious; the failure drill counts what one lost pod costs.

Learning outcomes

  1. Compute the HPA decision: ceil(replicas × metric / target), clamped to [min, max] — and why int() is the classic bug.
  2. Explain the inclusive ±10 % tolerance band as the guard against flapping.
  3. Read the 0/300 s windows as risk pricing: the maximum of recommendations over 20 ticks of 15 s.
  4. Choose a serving signal (queue, KV, TTFT) and argue why GPU utilization is the anti-signal.
  5. Run the failure drill: 2×250 → 250 tok/s on a kill; the 503 window at one replica; PDB vs minReplicas.
  6. Deliver the milestone: an HPA experiment, a capacity model (ledger × replicas) and incident notes.
