Glossary

Parallel processing of prompt tokens that builds initial KV state and produces the first-token logits.

Glossary

Roofline

Upper-bound performance model combining compute and bandwidth ceilings; not a full latency predictor.

Glossary

SLO

Service-level objective: a measurable reliability or performance target over a defined population and window.

Glossary

Tail latency

A high percentile such as p95 or p99, always meaningful only with its population and failure semantics.

Glossary

TPOT

Time per output token after the first, usually decode duration divided by post-first-token gaps; not one individual ITL.

Glossary

TTFT

Time from the declared request start boundary until the first output token becomes observable.

Why one order at a time wastes the stove

Why one order at a time wastes the stove Every decode step reads all the weights $$ t_{\text{step}} \;\gtrsim\; \frac{\text{bytes of weights}}{\text{memory bandwidth}} \;=\; \frac{8\ \text{GB}}{896\ \text{GB/s}} \;\approx\; 9\ \text{ms} $$ B = 1 One token per step for one request → ≤ ~110 tokens/s , no matter how fast the math units are. B = 64 The same weight read serves 64 requests → ceiling ~64× higher , until compute or memory runs out. ⇒ Batching is cheap in weight traffic during decode. Compute, KV traffic and scheduling still grow. Example: a 4B-parameter model in BF16 = 8 GB of weights; the judge GPU (RTX 5070 Ti) has about 896 GB/s of memory bandwidth. To produce one token, the GPU has to stream every weight from memory once: ≈ 9 ms. So a single request is capped near 110 tokens/s — the arithmetic units sit mostly idle, waiting for memory. If 64 requests decode in the same step, the weight traffic can be amortized across the batch. That does not make batching free: matrix compute, KV-cache reads and writes, scheduling work and memory capacity all grow, and the workload eventually becomes compute-bound or runs out of KV cache. This is a lower bound on step time and an upper bound on speed — real numbers are lower. Topic 2 does this properly (roofline, arithmetic intensity).

The catch: counter space

The catch: counter space Every live order keeps its KV cache in VRAM $$ \text{KV per token} \;=\; 2 \times L \times H_{kv} \times d_h \times \text{bytes} $$ 2 a key and a value vector per head \(L \cdot H_{kv} \cdot d_h\) layers × KV heads × head size: \(36 \times 8 \times 128\) for a 4B-class model = 144 KiB per token in FP16 (2 bytes) — kept for every token of every live request The arithmetic: 2 (K and V) × 36 layers × 8 KV heads × 128 head dimension × 2 bytes = 147 456 bytes ≈ 144 KiB per token. A representative 4B-class configuration; your model's config.json gives its own L, H_kv and d_h. The KV cache grows with every prompt token and every generated token, for every request that is in flight. Next slide: what that means inside 16 GB.

The big wok

The fix, in one picture Continuous batching: one wok, many orders. A new order joins at the next toss; a finished plate leaves at the next toss. Nobody waits for the whole table. Preview of topics 4–7. Static batching waits until a batch is full and until its longest answer is done. Continuous (iteration-level) batching re-forms the batch at every decode step: requests enter and leave the running batch individually (Orca, OSDI 2022; vLLM). PagedAttention (vLLM, SOSP 2023) is the counter split into small fixed trays, so no guest reserves space they never use. We do not explain them today — we just watch the effect.

How a round is scored

How a round is scored Gates first, then one number: S Step Rule Snapshot Thursday 23:59 MSK: the commit your round-NN tag points to. No tag → 0. Contract Server starts and lists submission within 30 min; a streamed smoke answer works. Quality gate GSM8K, IFEval, MMLU-Pro subsets (lm-eval): each ≥ 90% of the reference. Latency & honesty gates p95 TTFT among successful requests at 64 clients ≤ the round's SLO; errors ≤ 1%; every hidden length probe ≥ 95% of 256 tokens. Same model Every hidden at-rest/load pair must match; per-request length and output-diversity checks also pass. Speed \(S = \sqrt{\text{tok/s}_{32} \cdot \text{tok/s}_{64}}\) Mark 0–5 \(5 \times \min(1,\ S / S_{\text{bar}})\); any failed gate → 0. Round = 4% : points = mark × 0.8. Top to bottom, every round. The snapshot makes the deadline real. A server that does not start, or that got fast by getting dumb, scores zero — quality, latency, errors and layered hidden probes are independent gates. Length is checked per hidden response, not by the GuideLLM mean; same-model similarity uses the worst pair, not the average; diverse prompts must not collapse to one canned output. These are practical anti-gaming checks, not cryptographic proof, so raw artifacts and provenance are retained for audit. Among valid submissions only S matters: the geometric mean of throughput at 32 and 64 clients. The mark is proportional: half the bar = 2.5 of 5. Each round is worth 4% of the course grade (mark × 0.8); ten rounds = 40%.
